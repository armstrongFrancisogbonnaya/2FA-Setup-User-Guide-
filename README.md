How to Set Up Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)
A step-by-step guide to turning on 2FA for your application account
Prepared by Armstrong Francis Ogbonnaya | armstrongogbonnaya.official@gmail.com
Version 1.0 | October 2026

1. About this guide
A password on its own is not much protection. People reuse passwords, attackers guess them, and data
breaches leak them. Two-factor authentication (2FA) adds a second check after the password, so a stolen
password is no longer enough to get into your account.
This guide shows you how to switch on 2FA using an authenticator app, which is the method most
applications support and the one I recommend. The screens look a little different from one application to
another, but the steps follow the same order almost everywhere. Set aside about ten minutes and keep your
phone nearby.
2. How 2FA works
Once 2FA is on, signing in has two parts. First you enter something you know, which is your password. Then
you enter something you have, which is a six-digit code generated on your phone. The code changes every
30 seconds, so a code that someone copies or overhears is useless almost straight away.
Most applications offer more than one way to receive that second code. Here is how the common options
compare:

3. What you need before you start

• Your username and password for the application you want to protect.

• A smartphone with an internet connection and a working camera.

• An authenticator app installed from your phone's app store. Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator
and Authy are all reliable choices.

• Your phone's date and time set to automatic. The codes depend on accurate time, and a wrong clock is
the most common reason a code gets rejected.

• A pen and paper, or a password manager, to store your backup codes (explained in Step 6).
Tip: Start with your email account. If someone gets into your email, they can reset the passwords on almost
everything else you own.

Sign in and open your account settings
Log in to the application on a computer if you can, since it is easier to scan a QR code from a larger
screen. 
Click your profile picture or name, usually in a top corner, and choose Settings or My Account.

Find the security section
Look for a tab or menu called Security, Login & Security or Privacy and Security. Inside it, find the
option named Two-factor authentication, 2-step verification or Multi-factor authentication. They
all mean the same thing.

Start the setup and choose an authenticator app
Click Enable, Turn on or Get started. If the application asks you to choose a method, pick
Authenticator app. You may be asked to type your password again. That is normal and is there to
confirm it is really you.

Scan the QR code
A square QR code will appear on the screen. Open your authenticator app on your phone, tap the + or
Add account button, choose Scan a QR code and point the camera at the screen. If scanning does
not work, choose Enter a setup key in the app and type in the text key shown beneath the QR code.
Your account will now appear in the app with a six-digit code next to it.
Confirm with a code

Type the six-digit code currently shown in your authenticator app into the box on the setup page and
click Verify or Confirm. If it is rejected, wait for the code to refresh and try the new one. Codes expire
every 30 seconds, so it is easy to be a moment too late.

Save your backup codes
The application will now show a list of backup (or recovery) codes. Download them or copy them out
by hand and store them somewhere safe. Each code works once and lets you sign in if you lose your
phone.
Check that 2FA is switched on
Go back to the security page. You should see a status such as Enabled or On. Some applications
also send a confirmation email. If you receive one you do not recognise, change your password
straight away.

Important: Do not keep your backup codes only on the phone that runs your authenticator app. If that phone is
lost, you lose both together. A printed copy in a safe place, or an entry in a password manager, is a better choice.

5. Test that it works
Do not wait until you are locked out to find a problem. Test it now, while you are still signed in and everything
is fresh:

• Sign out of the application completely.

• Sign back in with your username and password.

• When prompted, open your authenticator app and enter the current six-digit code.

• If you land on your account page, setup is finished.

7. Good habits to keep
It may be hidden under another

• Never share a code. No genuine company or support agent will ask you to read one out.

• Moving to a new phone? Transfer your authenticator accounts before you wipe the old device. Most
apps have a transfer or export option.

• Use 2FA everywhere you can. Start with email, banking, social media and work accounts.

• Review your settings now and then. Remove devices you no longer use and generate new backup
codes if you have run low.
